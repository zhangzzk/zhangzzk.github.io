Zekang Zhang - Cosmology & AI

I'm a PhD student at the University Observatory Munich, LMU, in the Astrophysics, Cosmology and Artificial Intelligence group with Daniel Gruen, finishing in 2027. I work on photometric galaxy surveys: how galaxy images, colours, positions, redshift distributions, and selections become reliable measurements of cosmic structure.

I do: image simulations and systematics, photometric redshift and shear, cosmological inference from data, working with (deep) machine learning, analytical models, and emulators.

Research

I work on photometric galaxy surveys, along five themes: image simulation, redshift estimation, shear calibration, machine learning, and statistical inference.

  • Image simulation - how to fake a sky? Pixel-level forward modeling of galaxy surveys with full observational realism: blending, PSF, noise, detection, photometry, and selection effects.
  • Redshift estimation - the distance of galaxies. Characterising redshift distributions n(z), SOM-based colour-redshift calibration, and spatial variation of n(z) from observational systematics.
  • Shear calibration - galaxy light distorted by gravity. Weak-lensing shape measurement, shear response, PSF systematics, FORKLENS, and simulation-based shear inference with detection, selection and blending.
  • Machine learning - machines that compress expensive simulations into useful models. A CNN-based shape measurement, and a shear inference framework built on image simulations with normalizing flows, folding detection, selection and blending into one model.
  • Statistical inference - practical inference from data. Inferring cosmology from mock data when the data vector is biased by systematics: spatially varying observing conditions, their impact on two-point statistics, and what that means for combining clustering and lensing.
